Half the week with clients. Half the week on the data function.

One half is the agency's largest accounts. Strategy, quarterly reviews, pitch work and the media investment decisions behind them, held against revenue rather than platform metrics.

The other half is the data function. Most agencies bolt AI onto the edge of what they already do and call it transformation. I am building the layer underneath: consistent measurement, one definition of performance across paid and organic, and reporting senior people will actually make decisions on. Automation only compounds on top of that.

Every build carries a business case. Build cost, run cost, maintenance, and the billable hours it returns. A good share of what gets proposed does not clear that bar, and killing those is worth as much as shipping the rest. What does get built is built with the team who will own it, because a tool nobody adopts is a write-off.

The main one

An ad account reviewer that does the reading

A proper account review costs a specialist the better part of a day: hundreds of settings, thousands of keywords, and very little of it judgement. This does the reading in minutes, checks the 243 things a strong specialist would check across Google, Meta and Microsoft, and writes both the findings and the client deck. What it does not do is decide. That stays with the strategist, which is the only part of the job worth paying for.
